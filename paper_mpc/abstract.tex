Correlation clustering (equivalently, cluster editing) has strong
approximation algorithms and strong heuristics, but on large graphs nobody
can say how far a given clustering is from optimal: exact solvers stop at a
few hundred vertices, and the lower bounds used at scale so far, packings of
bad triangles, certify only a factor of about two.  We compute certified
lower bounds on sparse unweighted instances with up to $10^6$ vertices.
Since optimal clusters have diameter at most two, the metric LP and its
strengthening by star and local subgraph inequalities can be restricted to
the $O(\sum_v\deg(v)^2)$ pairs at distance at most two without losing
validity or LP-rounding guarantees.  Dual block-coordinate ascent,
warm-started by a star packing on these pairs, turns this relaxation into an
anytime bound that is valid after every step.  Its certificates are verified
against the raw instance file, in exact arithmetic, by a short checker that
shares no code with the solver; the inequality it evaluates is proved in
Lean~4.  The optimality gap of any clustering decomposes exactly into
non-negative local terms, which yields local optimality certificates.  On
the PACE~2021 exact track the checked bound proves optimality on
\numPaceOptOurs{} of 173 solved instances, against 79 for the root
star-packing bound of the KaPoCE branch-and-bound; the latter is stronger on
average (\numPaceMeanStar{} against \numPaceMeanOurs{} of the optimum) and on
the denser instances.  Our bound improves the published root bounds of two
open instances.  On \numSnapCert{} of \numSnapTotal{} SNAP graphs
(\numSnapCertHeld{} of \numSnapHeldTotal{} held out during development; the
others exceed a memory limit fixed in advance), our checked bounds certify
the archived solutions within \numSnapGapLo{} to \numSnapGapHi{} (median
\numSnapGapMedian{}); triangle packings leave \numBaseGapLo{} to
\numBaseGapHi{} (median \numBaseGapMedian{}).  On the graphs with more than
$2\cdot10^4$ vertices this bound comes from the star packing alone; the
block LPs improve it only on smaller or denser graphs.
