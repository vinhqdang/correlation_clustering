On large graphs, nobody can say how far a correlation clustering (cluster
editing) solution is from optimal: exact solvers stop at a few hundred
vertices, and the lower bounds used at scale, packings of bad triangles,
certify only factors of \numBaseFacLo{} to \numBaseFacHi{} on our graphs.
Optimal clusters have diameter at most two, so the metric LP, its star and
local subgraph inequalities, and every packing of induced stars can be
restricted to the pairs at distance at most two.  On these pairs we
reimplement the star-packing local search of the KaPoCE branch-and-bound
in memory linear in the graph and the packing, and add dual
block-coordinate ascent as an anytime bound.  Their bounds are exported
as certificates and verified against the raw instance, in exact arithmetic,
by an independent checker.  On the PACE~2021 exact track, under the
branch-and-bound's own stopping rule, the reimplementation proves optimality
on \numPaceKrOpt{} of the 173 solved instances, against 79 for the published
root bound; run for \SI{60}{s} it proves \numPaceSlsOpt{}, and the block LPs
close \numPaceCfOnlyKept{} more that longer packing does not.  We obtain
checked bounds on all \numSnapTotal{} SNAP graphs, with up to
\numMaxCertN{} vertices; on the \numSnapGapN{} with an archived clustering
they certify it within \numSnapGapLo{} to \numSnapGapHi{} (median
\numSnapGapMedian{}), where triangle packings leave \numBaseGapLo{} to
\numBaseGapHi{}.  Where the dense branch-and-bound code runs, its root bound
exceeds ours on \numRootBetter{} of seven graphs by at most
\numRootBetterHi{}, and its packings pass the same checker.
