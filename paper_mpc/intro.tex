\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

In \emph{correlation clustering}~\cite{BansalBC04} the input is a complete
graph whose pairs are labelled \emph{similar} ($+$) or \emph{dissimilar}
($-$), and the task is to partition the vertices so that the number of
disagreements, $+$ pairs between clusters plus $-$ pairs inside clusters, is
minimum.  The number of clusters is free.  Equivalently, given the positive
graph $G^+=(V,\Ep)$, one adds or deletes the fewest edges that turn $G^+$ into
a disjoint union of cliques (\emph{cluster editing}).  The problem is
APX-hard~\cite{CharikarGW05}.  It arises in entity resolution, community
detection and the consolidation of inconsistent pairwise information.

Two lines of work have progressed separately.  Approximation ratios for
complete graphs have fallen from $3$ for Pivot~\cite{AilonCN08} through
$2.06$ by rounding the metric LP~\cite{ChawlaMSY15} to $1.485$ with the
cluster LP~\cite{CaoCLLNV24} and below~\cite{GarciaSorianoS26}.  In practice,
engineered heuristics such as KaPoCE~\cite{BlasiusEtAl21kapoceHeur}, the
winner of the PACE~2021 challenge~\cite{KellerhalsKNZ21pace}, and multilevel
memetic solvers~\cite{hausberger2025scc} find very good solutions on large
graphs (for the latter, on sparse signed graphs with millions of vertices,
whose objective differs from ours; Section~\ref{sec:prelim}).  What is missing on such graphs is a
\emph{certificate}: a lower bound on the optimum, checkable independently of
the solver, that tells how far a given clustering can be from optimal.  Exact
branch-and-bound solves instances with a few hundred
vertices~\cite{BlasiusEtAl22sea}, and its strong packing bounds are
implemented on a dense $n\times n$ matrix.  Solvers for the metric LP of
correlation clustering on complete graphs reach about $10^4$
vertices~\cite{veldt2019metric,ruggles2020parallel}.  The combinatorial
bounds that do scale, packings of bad triangles, certify solutions only within
factors of \numBaseFacLo{} to \numBaseFacHi{} on the graphs of our study;
the LP bounds derived from strong triadic closure~\cite{veldt2022stc} are
supported on the same wedges.

This paper is about the lower-bound side.  We compute lower bounds for
correlation clustering on sparse graphs with up to
$\numMaxCertN{}$ vertices and $\numMaxCertM{}$ edges, export them as
certificates that an independent program checks in exact arithmetic against
the raw instance file, and use them to certify the solutions of our own and of
other solvers.  The primal methods are secondary; they are included because a
bound is only as useful as the solutions it is compared with, and because the
certificate yields a decomposition of the optimality gap that guides a local
search.

\paragraph{Contributions.}  The paper contributes an implementation that
makes the strongest combinatorial lower bound known for cluster editing, the
star packing of the KaPoCE branch-and-bound, run on graphs with $10^6$
vertices; a certificate format with an independent exact checker; and a
computational study of how strong each bound is and where its strength comes
from.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Distance-two support} (Section~\ref{sec:relax}).  Optimal
clusters have diameter at most two in $G^+$, so every pair at distance at
least three is separated in every optimal clustering; the KaPoCE
branch-and-bound uses this to forbid such pairs~\cite{BlasiusEtAl22sea}.  We
state the consequence for relaxations: the metric LP restricted to the
$O(\sum_v\deg(v)^2)$ pairs at distance at most two, with the triangle
inequalities through distance-three pairs kept as covering rows, is a
relaxation whose feasible points extend to the full metric LP at the same
cost (Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse}), and star and local subgraph inequalities
remain valid on it.  The observation is simple and its value is enabling:
the bounds, their dual solutions and their certificates are stored on these
pairs.  Every packing of induced stars lives there as well.

\item \textbf{Star local search on the support} (Section~\ref{sec:dual}).
The root bound of the KaPoCE branch-and-bound~\cite{BlasiusEtAl22sea} is a
star packing found by a local search that extends the $P_3$-packing search of
\citet{GottesburenHSSWZ20}; it is implemented on a dense $n\times n$ matrix.
We reimplement the same heuristic on the sparse graph, with a hash map over
the used pairs in place of the matrix, so that its memory is linear in the
graph plus the packing, and write its stars directly as certificate rows.
What is new is the scale: it runs on graphs with $10^6$ vertices, where the
dense implementation does not fit into memory.  It is not a better
heuristic.  With the stopping rule of the branch-and-bound, it proves
optimality on \numPaceKrOpt{} of the 173 solved PACE exact instances in a
median of \numPaceKrTimeMedian{}\,s, against 79 for the published root bound;
run for \SI{60}{s}, it proves optimality on \numPaceSlsOpt{}.  The published
root bounds are thus far from what the same heuristic reaches with a few
seconds more.

\item \textbf{Anytime block-dual bound} (Section~\ref{sec:dual}).  We compute
a lower bound by dual block-coordinate ascent, a relax-and-cut scheme: each
step solves the LP of one vertex block exactly by cutting planes, with costs
equal to the current reduced costs.  The bound is valid after every step and
never decreases, and its state is one reduced cost per pair plus the rows
with positive multipliers (Theorem~\ref{thm:anytime}).  Any star or triangle
packing is a feasible start.  After a strong star packing, and at an
equal nominal budget (with nearly equal measured times), it pays on the SNAP
graphs only where short cycles rather than
stars carry the bound (\numEqLpWinList{}; Section~\ref{sec:exp-cert}); on
the PACE exact track it closes \numPaceCfOnlyKept{} instances that the star
local search does not close with five times the budget or with five seeds.

\item \textbf{Exactly checked certificates} (Section~\ref{sec:cert}).  A
certificate lists the rows with positive multipliers.  A checker of about
\numCheckerLines{} lines that shares no code with the solver parses the raw
instance file, verifies that it is the instance the certificate was computed
for (SHA-256 of the file and of the canonical edge list), checks the validity
of every row by enumeration or by a closed form, and evaluates the bound in
integer arithmetic.  The inequality it evaluates, given valid rows, is proved
in Lean~4; the validity procedures are tested, not verified.  The format is
specified independently of the code, and the checker also accepts the
packings of the KaPoCE branch-and-bound, exported from its code.  We archive
every certificate behind every checked bound we report.

\item \textbf{Computational study} (Section~\ref{sec:exp}).  On the PACE~2021
exact track, the checked bounds improve the published root bounds of
\numPaceOpenImproved{} of the \numPaceOpen{} open instances.  On all
\numSnapTotal{} SNAP graphs we obtain checked bounds; on the \numSnapGapN{}
with an archived clustering, the largest checked bound certifies it within
\numSnapGapLo{} to \numSnapGapHi{} (median \numSnapGapMedian{}), where
triangle packings leave \numBaseGapLo{} to \numBaseGapHi{}.  We report the
spread of the star local search over five seeds and its progress over time,
compare it at equal nominal budget with the block LPs, and compare with the triangle
packings, the metric LP on the support with triangle or star rows, a
multicut dual solver, and the root bound of the branch-and-bound where it
runs.
\end{enumerate}

The certificate also yields a decomposition of the optimality gap into
non-negative local terms (Section~\ref{sec:gap}), which the primal method
CertiFlip uses to choose the neighbourhoods of a large-neighbourhood search;
this choice is not evaluated separately, and we do not count it as a
contribution.  A second primal solver, PXMem (Appendix~\ref{sec:primal}),
recombines two clusterings exactly block by block of their overlap; its
comparison with KaPoCE under a protocol fixed in advance, which finds no
significant difference at \SI{600}{s}, is in Appendix~\ref{sec:exp-h2h}.  The
upper bounds against which the certificates are measured are the best
archived clusterings of all solvers, KaPoCE included: checked lower bounds
exist for all \numSnapTotal{} SNAP graphs, certified gaps for the
\numSnapGapN{} with an archived clustering.

\paragraph{Relation to certified computation.}  Certifying
algorithms~\cite{McConnellMNS11} return a witness with every output.  Safe
bounds from floating-point LP solutions~\cite{NeumaierS04safe,SteffyW13valid},
checkable certificates for mixed-integer programming~\cite{CheungGS17vipr}
and proof logging in SAT and pseudo-Boolean solving~\cite{WetzlerHH14drat,
GochtN21veripb} pursue the same goal.  Our certificates are simpler than
general MIP certificates because every row is local: its validity is decided
by enumerating the partitions of at most nine vertices or by a closed form
(Section~\ref{sec:cert} compares them with VIPR and pseudo-Boolean proof
logging).

\paragraph{Development and evaluation data.}  The methods were developed on
fifteen SNAP graphs and the PACE~2021 exact track.  Before the final primal
experiments we fixed a held-out set of twelve further SNAP graphs and an
analysis plan for the comparison with KaPoCE (Appendix~\ref{sec:exp-h2h}).
The bound procedures were not evaluated out of sample: the star local search
was written after results on all graphs were known and was tried on seven
held-out graphs during its development, so we report the SNAP bounds as one
benchmark and keep the distinction between development and held-out graphs
only as a description.
Appendix~\ref{app:devlog} records which data informed which design decision.
