\paragraph{Certified gaps.}  Every one of the \numSnapTotal{} graphs now has
a checked lower bound (Table~\ref{tab:snap-bounds}).  On the \numSnapGapN{}
graphs with an archived clustering, the largest checked bound certifies it
within \numSnapGapLo{} to \numSnapGapHi{} (median \numSnapGapMedian{}); on
the other \numSnapNoArch{} (\numSnapNoArchList{}) only gap$^*$, against the
best value of any run, is available.  The triangle packings, the lower
bounds used at this scale so far, leave gaps of \numBaseGapLo{} to
\numBaseGapHi{} (median \numBaseGapMedian{}) on the graphs with a support.
The median gap is \numSnapGapMedDev{} on the development graphs and
\numSnapGapMedHeld{} on the held-out graphs.  We attribute most of the
difference to the composition of the held-out set, which contains the
denser social and citation networks and four of the five largest graphs,
but with twelve held-out graphs we cannot exclude that the methods are
tuned to the development graphs.  The largest gaps are on
\texttt{web-NotreDame} (\numSnapGapHi{}), a web graph with hubs of more than
$10^4$ links, and on the held-out voting, citation and e-mail graphs
\texttt{wiki-Vote}, \texttt{cit-HepPh} and \texttt{email-EuAll}; the column
gap$^*$ shows that no solver we ran closes them from above, so we cannot
tell how they divide between the two bounds.

\paragraph{The star local search.}  The star local search gives the largest
checked bound on \numBestFromSls{} of the \numSnapTotal{} graphs, alone or
followed by block LPs.  It raises the best bound of our earlier procedures
on \numSlsBetter{} graphs, is below it on \numSlsBelow{}
(\numSlsBelowList{}, sparse graphs on which the block LPs of CertiFlip gain
most), and gives the only bound on \numSlsNew{} (\numSlsNewList{}), where the
earlier implementation of the packing materialised the support and ran out
of memory.  The largest change is on \numSlsTopGraph{}, whose gap falls from
\numSlsTopOld{} to \numSlsTopNew{}; over the \numSnapGapOldN{} graphs that
had a checked bound and an archived clustering before, the median gap falls
from \numSnapGapOldMedian{} to \numSnapGapSameMedian{}.  A run took a median of
\numSlsTimeMedian{}\,s and at most \numSlsTimeMax{}\,s, including the
certificate: the time limit is checked between stars, and the stars are
written as certificate rows directly, without building the support.  On
\numSlsKmaxList{}, whose largest degrees are $2.9\cdot10^4$ and
$7.6\cdot10^3$, the unrestricted search was killed three times for lack of
memory (a star with $k$ leaves occupies $k+\binom k2$ pairs); with at most
100 leaves per star it used about \SI{4}{GB} and finished.

\paragraph{Block LPs after the star local search.}  Starting the block LPs
from the packing of the star local search (column SLS+LP, half of the
\SI{1200}{s} for each phase) raised the bound above that packing on
\numSlpGainPos{} of the \numSlpN{} graphs with a support, by a median of
\numSlpGainMedian{} and by at most \numSlpGainMax{} on
\numSlpGainMaxGraph{}, whose road network has many short cycles and few
large induced stars.  With the ruin-and-recreate packing as the start the
block LPs had added a median of \numPlpGainMedian{} (column R\&R+LP), because
that packing leaves more room.  With the stronger packing the relaxation
adds little on the social and collaboration graphs; the gaps that remain are
those of the star packing bound itself or of the clusterings.  The earlier
comparison of the two uses of the same budget with the ruin-and-recreate
packing is in Appendix~\ref{app:eqtime}.

\paragraph{The root bound of the branch-and-bound.}  On the graphs with at
most $2.5\cdot10^4$ vertices, the root star-packing bound of the KaPoCE
branch-and-bound finished within four hours on \numRootN{} graphs
(Table~\ref{tab:snap-bounds}).  It exceeds all of our checked bounds on
\numRootBetter{} of them (\numRootBetterList{}), by \numRootBetterLo{} to
\numRootBetterHi{}, and is below them on the others (\numRootBelowList{}),
by at most \numRootBelowMax{}.  Its star bound took \numRootTimeLo{}\,s to
\numRootTimeHi{}\,h, against at most \numSlsTimeMax{}\,s for the star local
search.  On \numRootTimeoutList{} it did not finish within the four hours,
and on \numRootMemList{} it needs about \SI{11.6}{GB} and was killed in each
of four runs on the machines with \SI{12.7}{GB}, the longest after
\SI{3.8}{h}; its dense matrices, of which it keeps several with $n^2$
entries, do not fit into memory above $2.5\cdot10^4$ vertices.  The two
implement the same heuristic, and the small differences where both finish
are those of a randomised local search stopped at different times.  Its
packings use the same rows as ours, so we exported them from its code (a
function of 13 lines that writes the stars of its final packing, centre
first; \texttt{experiments/kapoce/}), converted each into a dual solution on
$P$ and ran our checker on it.  The checker accepted all \numRootCert{}, each
with the value KaPoCE reports: the same certificate format and checker
certify the bounds of another solver.

\paragraph{Strength of the relaxation.}  Table~\ref{tab:snap-lp} separates
the strength of the relaxation from the way it is solved.  On
\texttt{ca-GrQc} the metric LP on $P$ with triangle rows converges in
\numGrqcTriTime{}\,s to \numGrqcTri{}, \numGrqcTriBelow{} below the best
known value (which is \numGrqcTriGap{} above it).  With star rows the LP reaches \numGrqcStar{}, \numGrqcStarGap{} below
the best known value and slightly above the best checked bound
(\numGrqcBest{}), after \numGrqcStarTime{}\,s.  The triangle inequalities,
which is what the classical metric LP has, are therefore far from sufficient
on this graph; the star inequalities close almost all of the gap, and the
anytime dual reaches nearly the same value in a tenth of the time.
RAMA, a dual method for the multicut LP with cycle inequalities, run with
its default parameters, stays below $\mathrm{LP}_P$ with triangle rows on
every graph where that converged, consistent with the argument of
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}.  Its values are not converged, however: they are
below the better triangle packing, a feasible dual of the same cycle LP, on
\numRamaBelowTri{} of the \numRamaOk{} graphs where it ran.  Its bound is at most
\numRamaFracMax{} of the best checked bound (on \numRamaFracMaxGraph{}); on
\numRamaNearZero{} graphs (\numRamaNearZeroList{}) it returned the trivial
bound, zero up to its printing precision (six significant digits, which is
up to $\pm50$ on \texttt{email-Enron}); and it stopped with a CUDA
illegal-address error on \numRamaFailed{} graphs.
