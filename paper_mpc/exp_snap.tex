\paragraph{Certified gaps.}  On the \numSnapCert{} graphs whose support fits
into memory, the larger of the two checked bounds certifies the archived
clusterings within \numSnapGapLo{} to \numSnapGapHi{} (median
\numSnapGapMedian{}; Table~\ref{tab:snap-bounds}).  The triangle packings,
the lower bounds that scale without the support, only certify factors of
\numBaseFacLo{} to \numBaseFacHi{} on the same graphs, which is the regime
of the MatchFlipPivot guarantee.  Across the three CertiFlip seeds the bound
varies by at most \numSnapSpreadMax{}.  The largest gaps are on the held-out
social and citation graphs \texttt{facebook}, \texttt{email-Eu-core},
\texttt{wiki-Vote} and \texttt{cit-HepPh}; the column gap$^*$ shows that no
solver we ran closes them from above, so they are due to the lower bound, the
upper bound, or both, and we cannot tell which.  The median gap is
\numSnapGapMedDev{} on the development graphs and \numSnapGapMedHeld{} on the
held-out graphs.  We attribute the difference mainly to the composition of the
held-out set, which contains the denser social and citation networks, but
with seven held-out graphs we cannot exclude that the method is tuned to the
development graphs.

\paragraph{Star packing against the block LPs.}  The sparse star packing
alone gives a larger checked bound than the CertiFlip bound on
\numPackBetter{} of the \numSnapCert{} graphs, although the packing run took a
median of \numPackTimeMedian{}\,s (\numPackTimeLo{} to \numPackTimeHi{}\,s;
its number of ruin-and-recreate iterations is fixed in advance from a
measured iteration rate) and the CertiFlip bound a median of
\numCfLbTimeMedian{}\,s.  The CertiFlip bound is larger only on
\numCfBetterList{}, which are small or dense enough for the block LPs to
converge.  Within CertiFlip the packing phase is short, and on large sparse
graphs the block LPs, started from a weaker packing, do not make up the
difference in the remaining time.  On these graphs the certified gap is
therefore due to the packing; the LP part of the method matters on the dense
graphs and on the PACE instances (Section~\ref{sec:exp-bounds}).  Running a
long packing first and the block LPs afterwards is the obvious combination.
We did not tune or evaluate it before fixing the held-out protocol and do not
report it.  The gaps of Table~\ref{tab:snap-bounds} use the better of the two
certificates, which were computed in two separate runs.

\paragraph{Strength of the relaxation.}  Table~\ref{tab:snap-lp} separates
the strength of the relaxation from the way it is solved.  On
\texttt{ca-GrQc} the metric LP on $P$ with triangle rows converges in
\numGrqcTriTime{}\,s to \numGrqcTri{}, \numGrqcTriGap{} below the best known
value.  With star rows the LP reaches \numGrqcStar{}, \numGrqcStarGap{} below
the best known value and slightly above the best checked bound
(\numGrqcBest{}), after \numGrqcStarTime{}\,s.  The triangle inequalities,
which is what the classical metric LP has, are therefore far from sufficient
on this graph; the star inequalities close almost all of the gap, and the
anytime dual reaches nearly the same value in a tenth of the time.
