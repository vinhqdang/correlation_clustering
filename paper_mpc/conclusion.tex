\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

Lower bounds that scale to large correlation clustering instances, packings
of bad triangles, certify only factors of \numBaseFacLo{} to \numBaseFacHi{}
on our graphs.  The stronger star packings of exact solvers were limited by a
dense implementation.  Because every star packing, and the metric LP with its
strengthenings, can be restricted to the pairs at distance at most two, the
star-packing heuristic of the KaPoCE branch-and-bound runs with memory linear
in the graph and the packing, and a dual block-coordinate ascent turns the
restricted relaxation into an anytime bound that starts from its packing.
This gives checked bounds on all SNAP graphs of our study, up to $10^6$
vertices.  The bounds are exported as certificates that an independent
checker verifies against the raw instance in exact arithmetic; the same
checker accepts the packings of the branch-and-bound.

On the PACE exact track the reimplementation matches the published root
bounds under the branch-and-bound's own stopping rule.  The larger number of
optimality proofs at \SI{60}{s} comes from running the same heuristic longer,
not from a better heuristic; the block LPs add a few instances on which more
packing time does not help.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The star local search implements the heuristic of an existing solver;
what is new is that it runs on the support, with its certificate, at a scale
where the dense implementation does not.  Where both finish, on seven SNAP
graphs, the root bound of the branch-and-bound, run for up to four hours,
exceeds ours on \numRootBetter{} of them by at most \numRootBetterHi{}.
\item The search stops on wall-clock time, so a seed does not fix its
result; over five seeds its bound varies by up to \numSlsSeedSpreadMax{} on
the SNAP graphs.  On the largest graphs it completes only a few rounds in
\SI{600}{s}, and with \SI{3600}{s} the bound rises by up to
\numSlsLongGainMax{}; the gaps on these graphs are partly a matter of budget.
\item At an equal budget the block LPs beat the star local search alone on
only \numEqLpWins{} of the \numEqLpN{} SNAP graphs with a support
(\numEqLpWinList{}); on the PACE exact track they close
\numPaceCfOnlyKept{} instances that five times the packing time does not.
Elsewhere the time is better spent on the packing.  We did not
optimise how to split a budget between the two phases, and we did not try
first-order LP methods, whose duals the checker would accept as well.
\item The remaining gaps on the social, citation and web graphs (up to
\numSnapGapHi{}) are not closed from either side, and we cannot tell how they
divide between the lower and the upper bound.
\item A star with $k$ leaves occupies $k+\binom k2$ pairs.  On the two graphs
with the largest hubs we capped the stars at 100 leaves to fit into
\SI{12.7}{GB}.
\item We consider unweighted instances only, and compute the bounds on the
input graph.  Twin contraction and other exact reductions are used on the
primal side only; bounds on the reduced, weighted instance would need the
distance-two rule in a weighted form and a checker that verifies the
reduction.
\item The checker is short but not formally verified; the formal development
covers the inequality it evaluates, not the procedures that decide the
validity of a row (Appendix~\ref{app:lean}).
\item The gap-guided choice of LNS neighbourhoods is not evaluated
separately.  The a-priori guarantees of our primal methods are inherited
from Pivot, and the $2.06$ guarantee applies only when one block covers the
graph and separation converged.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open directions.}  Using the stronger root bound inside the
branch-and-bound; lower bounds on reduced instances; $K_{a,b}$ packings, which
\citet{BlasiusEtAl22sea} found comparable to stars; the split of the budget
between packing and block LPs; first-order and parallel block solves;
cluster-LP columns inside blocks, to which the roundings of
\citet{CaoCLLNV24} and \citet{GarciaSorianoS26} could be applied; and a
verified checker, for example by extracting it from the Lean development or
by translating the rows into a proof format with a verified checker.
