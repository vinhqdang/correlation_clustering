\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

Lower bounds that scale to large correlation clustering instances, packings
of bad triangles, certify only factors of about two.  On sparse graphs, three
observations give much tighter checked bounds: the metric LP, its
strengthenings and every star packing can be restricted to the pairs at
distance at most two; a local search for star packings, the heuristic
behind the root bound of the branch-and-bound, runs on these pairs with
memory linear in the graph and the packing; and dual block-coordinate ascent
turns the restricted relaxation into an anytime bound that starts from any
packing.  With \SI{60}{s} per instance the star local search is stronger
than the published root bound on the PACE exact track, and it gives checked
bounds on all SNAP graphs of our study, up to $10^6$ vertices.  The bounds
are exported as certificates that an independent checker verifies against
the raw instance in exact arithmetic; the same checker accepts the packings
of the branch-and-bound.  The gap decomposition turns a certificate into a
map of where a clustering can still improve.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The star local search implements the heuristic of an existing
solver; what is new is that it runs on the support, with its certificate,
at a scale where the dense implementation does not.  Where both finish, on
seven SNAP graphs, the root bound of the branch-and-bound, run for up to
four hours, exceeds ours on \numRootBetter{} of them by at most
\numRootBetterHi{} (Section~\ref{sec:exp-cert}).
\item After a strong star packing the block LPs add a median of
\numSlpGainMedian{} on the SNAP graphs; they matter on sparse graphs with
many short cycles, such as \texttt{roadNet-PA}, and on the PACE instances
below density $0.1$.  We did not optimise how to split a budget between the
two phases.
\item The remaining gaps on the held-out social, citation and web graphs
(up to \numSnapGapHi{}) are not closed from either side, and we cannot tell
how they divide between the lower and the upper bound.
\item A star with $k$ leaves occupies $k+\binom k2$ pairs.  On the two graphs
with the largest hubs we capped the stars at 100 leaves to fit into
\SI{12.7}{GB}.
\item We consider unweighted instances only.  The weighted form enters only
through the contraction of twins in the primal methods.
\item The checker is short but not formally verified; the formal development
covers the inequality it evaluates, not its code (Appendix~\ref{app:lean}).
\item The gap-guided choice of LNS neighbourhoods is not evaluated
separately.  The a-priori guarantees of our primal methods are inherited
from Pivot, and the $2.06$ guarantee applies only when one block covers the
graph and separation converges.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open directions.}  Star packings with weights, which the same
local search could handle with multiplicities; the split of the budget
between packing and block LPs; parallel block solves; cluster-LP columns
inside blocks, to which the roundings of \citet{CaoCLLNV24} and
\citet{GarciaSorianoS26} could be applied; overlapping blocks with
Lagrangian cost splitting; and a verified checker, for example by
extracting it from the Lean development.
