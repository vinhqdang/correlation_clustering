\paragraph{Same heuristic, different stopping rules.}  The published root
bound of the KaPoCE branch-and-bound and our star local search pack the same
induced stars with the same moves; they differ in the implementation and in
when they stop.  The published data state neither the time spent on the root
bounds nor whether they were computed after the data reductions of the
branch-and-bound, so we reran its lower-bound program on the input graphs,
without reductions (the program of Section~\ref{sec:exp-cert}, \SI{900}{s}
limit, one run per instance; \numKrootPaceColab{} on the Colab machines, one
at a time, the others on a 4-core cloud machine with a \SI{2.2}{GHz} Xeon, at
most three at a time).  The star packing finished within the limit on
\numKrootPaceStarN{} of the \numKrootPaceN{} instances
(\numKrootPaceTimeoutList{} stopped after the $P_3$ packing).  Its $P_3$
packing equals the published value on \numKrootPacePthreeEqual{} of
\numKrootPacePthreeN{}, its star packing on \numKrootPaceStarEqual{}, and on
the others the star packing differs by at most \numKrootPaceStarDiffMax{}
(\numKrootPaceStarAbove{} larger, \numKrootPaceStarBelow{} smaller, at most
\numKrootPaceStarRelMax{}; the heuristic uses a random generator, which we
ran with its default seed, and the published values may come from another
version).  The published root bounds are therefore bounds of the input
graphs.  The heuristic stops by its own rule, once the rounds are more than
five times the rounds that improved the packing; its star bound took a median
of \numKrootPaceTimeMedian{}\,s (at most \numKrootPaceTimeMax{}\,s).

Under the same rule, with \SI{60}{s} only as a cap, the star local search
proves optimality on \numPaceKrOpt{} of the 173 solved instances (mean LB/OPT
\numPaceKrMean{}), against 79 for the published root bound (mean
\numPaceMeanStar{}); it is larger than that bound on \numPaceKrAbove{}
instances, equal on \numPaceKrEqual{} and smaller on \numPaceKrBelow{}, and
took a median of \numPaceKrTimeMedian{}\,s including its certificate
(Table~\ref{tab:pace-bounds}); \numPaceKrCap{} runs reached the cap.  Over five seeds its count ranges from
\numPaceKrOptSeedLo{} to \numPaceKrOptSeedHi{}.  The reimplementation is thus
equivalent to the original where both run, which is what it was written to
be.  Run until its \SI{60}{s} time limit instead, the same search proves
optimality on \numPaceSlsOpt{} instances (mean \numPaceSlsMean{}; from
\numPaceSlsOptSeedLo{} to \numPaceSlsOptSeedHi{} over five seeds, mean
\numPaceSlsMeanSeedLo{} to \numPaceSlsMeanSeedHi{}), and at least
\numPaceSlsMin{} of the optimum on every instance.  On the
\numPaceSlsOverKrOpt{} instances that it closes only with the longer run, the
\SI{60}{s} run reached the optimum after a median of
\numPaceReachOptMedian{}\,s.  The published root bounds are therefore far
from what the same heuristic reaches with a few seconds more; the dense
implementation could be run longer on these instances as well, and our
comparison says nothing about which implementation is faster per round.
Our earlier ruin-and-recreate packing reaches only \numPacePackMean{} on
average.

\paragraph{Block LPs.}  The CertiFlip bound, a ruin-and-recreate packing
followed by block LPs within a \SI{60}{s} CertiFlip run, proves optimality on
\numPaceOptOurs{} instances and is larger than the star local search on
\numPaceSlsUnderCf{}, by one to eight units.  On \numPaceCfOnly{} of these it
is optimal and the star local search (seed~0, \SI{60}{s}) is not.  Neither
four further seeds nor a run of \SI{300}{s} close \numPaceCfOnlyKept{} of them
(closed: \numPaceCfOnlyClosedList{}), and their densities range from
\numPaceCfOnlyDensLo{} to \numPaceCfOnlyDensHi{}: there the block LPs close
units that more packing time does not.  Together, the two checked bounds
prove optimality on \numPaceBestOpt{} instances (mean \numPaceBestMean{}), at
a cost of two runs of \SI{60}{s} nominal each; adding the published root bound
to them proves optimality on only one more (\numPaceBestMaxOpt{}).  Below
density $0.1$ the two checked bounds are equal on average
(Table~\ref{tab:pace-density}).

On every fourth instance we also ran the bound with \SI{1800}{s} and one
block covering the graph, and the metric LP on $P$ with triangle rows only.
With the longer budget the mean LB/OPT on these \numPaceWarmN{} instances is
\numPaceWarmMean{}, against \numPaceWarmOursMean{} with the \SI{60}{s} runs
and \numPaceWarmStarMean{} for the B\&B star packing, and the bound is
optimal on \numPaceWarmOpt{} of them (\numPaceWarmOursOpt{} at \SI{60}{s}).
The triangle LP converged within \SI{900}{s} on \numPaceTriN{} of these
instances, with a mean LB/OPT of \numPaceTriMean{} (\numPaceTriOursMean{}
for our bound on the same instances).  Of the \numWarmRuns{} bound runs,
\numWarmLocal{} were made on a laptop (\numCpuLocal), several at a time, and one
on a Colab machine.  The limit applies to the LP phase; the star-packing warm
start before it (sized to about 30\% of the limit) and the certificate come
on top, and \numWarmOver{} of the \numWarmRuns{} runs took longer than
\SI{1800}{s}, the longest \numWarmMax{}\,s.  The checker accepted all their
certificates.

\paragraph{Primal quality.}  On the exact track the primal side is not the
