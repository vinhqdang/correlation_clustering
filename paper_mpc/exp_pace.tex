\paragraph{Strength of the bounds.}  With at most \SI{30}{s} for the bound,
the checked CertiFlip bound proves optimality on \numPaceOptOurs{} of the 173
solved instances (Table~\ref{tab:pace-bounds}); the root star packing of the
KaPoCE branch-and-bound does so on 79 and its $P_3$ packing on 59.  On
average, however, the B\&B star packing is stronger (mean LB/OPT
\numPaceMeanStar{} against \numPaceMeanOurs{}); our bound is larger on
\numPaceOursAbove{} instances, smaller on \numPaceOursBelow{} and equal on
\numPaceOursEqual{}.  The two bounds are complementary: ours is tight on many
instances and loose on some, the B\&B star packing is tight less often and
is never far off.  Table~\ref{tab:pace-density} shows that the comparison
depends on density.  Below density $0.1$ our bound is the stronger one; on
denser instances the support contains most pairs, the block LPs are large and
do not converge within the budget, and the star packing of the
branch-and-bound wins.  The sparse star packing alone reaches a mean of
\numPacePackMean{} and proves optimality on \numPacePackOpt{} of
\numPacePackN{} instances.  It is weakest on the dense instances
(Table~\ref{tab:pace-density}), where its local search over the support is
much weaker than the packings of the branch-and-bound, which work on the full
matrix; there the block LPs supply almost all of the CertiFlip bound.  This is
the opposite of what we observe on the large sparse SNAP graphs
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-cert}).

On every fourth instance we also ran the bound with \SI{1800}{s} and one
block covering the graph, and the metric LP on $P$ with triangle rows only.
With the longer budget the mean LB/OPT on these \numPaceWarmN{} instances is
\numPaceWarmMean{}, against \numPaceWarmOursMean{} with the \SI{60}{s} runs
and \numPaceWarmStarMean{} for the B\&B star packing, and the bound is
optimal on \numPaceWarmOpt{} of them (\numPaceWarmOursOpt{} at \SI{60}{s}).
The triangle LP converged within \SI{900}{s} on \numPaceTriN{} of these
instances, with a mean LB/OPT of \numPaceTriMean{} (\numPaceTriOursMean{}
for our bound on the same instances).

\paragraph{Primal quality.}  On the exact track the primal side is not the
bottleneck (Table~\ref{tab:pace-primal}).  CertiFlip finds the best known
value on \numCfHit{} of the 200 instances although it spends up to half of
its \SI{60}{s} on the bound; SCC on the complete encoding and KaPoCE find it
on all of them.  The certified gaps on this track are therefore determined by
the bound.
