## 0. Verdict: SOUND WITH FIXES (no gap in the logic of Theorem S or Lemma 5; three wording fixes, one missing detail)

I found no cheaper way to satisfy a NO instance. In particular the proof does not assume tunnel-release semantics in the soundness direction: Theorem S derives it from Lemma 5 + decomposition lemma + Lemma 6 + the region count, for arbitrary edit sets. The independent exact computation (Sec. 3) on the full thick reduction for k = 1, 2, 3 agrees with `ex = k^2` on YES and `ex >= k^2 + 1` on NO.

Fixes (none changes a statement that is used):
* F1 (wording, same as audit 14 F1). Lemma 5 and the first line of the proof of Theorem S say "no pair inside a super-vertex is edited". False: the host pairs `(h,i)(h,k)` are soft NON-edges inside `K_h`, and every ZE partition inserts them (state M, the single edit of the host member). What is true and what is used: every `K_v` is inside one cluster, hence no hard glue pair is edited and no comparator pad pair (a soft edge inside `K_v`) is edited. Reword.
* F2 (missing detail in the arithmetic of Theorem S). The inequality `|S cap Pi_c| >= |H_c| + max(1,h_c)` drops the member-excess slack `E_c` (members of `H_c` with two or three edits). The equality analysis at `ex = k^2` needs `E_c = 0`. It follows because every inequality in the chain is tight at `ex = k^2` (all slacks are non-negative), but the text should define `r_c := |S cap Pi_c| - |H_c| = h_c + E_c >= 1`, redo the sum with `r_c`, and say "tightness forces `E_c = 0`, `|S_rest| = |H_cmp|`". With `r_c` the whole counting is: `ex = sum r_c - q + (|S_rest| - |H_cmp|)`, `sum h_c = h_in + 2q`, `r_c >= max(1,h_c)`.
* F3 (definition). `h_c` is defined as "edits on pairs of no member"; it should read "of no member of `H_c`" (a pad pair of an `H_cmp` member lies in `Pi_c`). It is harmless because Lemma 5 forbids edits on those pad pairs, but the definition as written is ambiguous.
* F4 (u_max). "u_max <= 3 by construction" has no count in the text. Count (also computed, Sec. 4): a host carries host-matching + lock-guest pad (tile group) = 2, or host-matching + the two comparator guests of a separator = 3; non-host super-vertices carry at most 2 pads. `a15_umax.py`, thin-guest model, all tiles present, k = 1..5, n = 2..4: u_max = 2 for k <= 2, 3 for k >= 3 (argmax is a separator host `s_{4u+2}`), never more. The second part of F4 in audit 14 (pw proof) I confirm below (Sec. 4).

## 1. Re-derivation of Theorem S (from scratch, nothing taken from the notes' text of the proof)

Let S be a solution, `ex(S) = |S| - |H| <= k^2 = l`, p even, `p >= k^2 + 5`.

1. Decomposition lemma: members are pair-disjoint (the `Builder` asserts "pair reuse" on every member), each has an edit, `|S| >= |H| + |U|` with U = edits on pairs of no member, so `|U| <= ex <= l`.
2. Lemma 5 (checked in detail). Suppose `K_v` meets two clusters; pick a part X of size `x <= p/2` and Y = rest. Edges between X and Y inside `K_v` are deleted. Non-hard pairs inside `K_v` are only pad/host pairs, at most u_max per vertex. So at least `x(p - x - u_max)` HARD edges are edited; this is concave in x, minimum at x = 1 (value `p-1-u_max`) or `x = p/2` (value `p/2 (p/2 - u_max)`), both `> l`: for `l = k^2, p >= k^2+5, u_max <= 3` we have `p - 1 - u_max >= k^2+1`. Contradiction with `|U| <= l`. Correct. (Hard glue pairs: all pairs of `K_v` except pad/host pairs, builder asserts glue connected.) Consequence: no hard glue pair and no comparator pad pair is edited (F1).
3. Regions. `R_c` = thick vertices of chain c and its guests `z,y,x` (not the comparator guests y_cmp); `T_c` = anchors of the partner chain c' (all anchors of c' have their unique tunnel to c); `Pi_c` = pairs inside `R_c cup T_c` minus pairs inside `T_c`. I rechecked the overlap by set algebra: for `c'' notin {c, c'}` we have `(R_c cup T_c) cap (R_{c''} cup T_{c''}) = empty` (T_c is in `R_{c'}`, partner is an involution); for `c'' = c'` the intersection is `T_c cup T_{c'}` (the anchors of c' and of c) and the only pairs left in both `Pi` are anchor(c)-anchor(c') pairs, i.e. exactly the cross-anchor pairs (the pairs inside `T_c` are excluded from `Pi_c` and live only in `Pi_{c'}`). So `|S| = sum_c |S cap Pi_c| - q + |S_rest|` is exact. Pairs of `H_c` members all lie in `Pi_c`; the only member pairs lying in `Pi_c` but not belonging to `H_c` are comparator pad pairs (unedited by Lemma 5). The y-pairs of pad members and the switch member `(z1,y,z2)` lie in no `Pi` (y is in no R, `z1 z2` joins two different non-partner chains: comparators join `A_ij`-`A_{i,j+1}` and `B_ij`-`B_{i+1,j}`, never partners). Hence `|S_rest| >= |H_cmp|`.
4. Lemma 6. If `|S cap Pi_c| = |H_c|` then one edit per `H_c` member, none elsewhere in `Pi_c`; the restriction to `R_c cup T_c` is a ZE partition of `G_c` (stub-stub pairs: two stubs together would make their anchors together across an unedited hard non-edge). Then Lemma 1 (chain is `P_a`), Lemma 3 (blow-up), Lemma 2 (host profile): every anchor x is in `{x,stub}` so lock member `(x,y,z)` is in state L, y with z with `K_host`, position outside the group; permanent locks cover the other groups; groups partition `[0,m)`. Contradiction. So `r_c >= 1`. This argument only uses the part of the partition inside `R_c cup T_c`, so whatever the rest of S does (comparators, cross-region edits, merges) cannot help the chain: this is why no double counting through other regions is possible.
5. Counting. `ex = sum_c r_c - q + (|S_rest|-|H_cmp|)`, `r_c = h_c + E_c >= max(1,h_c)`, `sum_c h_c = h_in + 2q` (a cross-anchor edit lies in the `Pi` of both chains and in no member), `Z = {c: h_c = 0}`; `#{c notin Z} <= sum h_c`. So `ex >= |Z| + h_in + q + sum E_c` and `|Z| >= 2k^2 - h_in - 2q`, whence `ex >= 2k^2 - q`; also `ex >= q` since each edited cross-anchor pair is hard. `ex >= max(q, 2k^2-q) >= k^2`. Verified algebraically. Regions that share pairs: the only shared pairs are the q cross-anchor ones and they are charged once to ex, twice to the chains, which is exactly what the formula does.
6. Equality `ex = k^2`: `q = k^2`, `Z = empty`, `h_in = 0`, `sum E_c = 0`, `S_rest` = one edit per `H_cmp` member, each chain has exactly one non-member edit (since `sum h_c = 2k^2`, `h_c >= 1`) and it is a cross-anchor pair. An inserted cross-anchor pair `(x_A(g), x_B(g'))`, `g' != g`, plus the unedited tunnel `x_A(g) x_B(g)` would put two B anchors together across an unedited hard non-edge (all of `Pi_B` except the single cross edit is unedited): impossible. So it is a tunnel deletion; one per chain, tunnels join `A_ij` and `B_ij`, so one per cell. S is a ZE partition of `I - C`, and Prop. 4 (=>) applies: the chain block of cell (i,j) is the block of the cut tile; the same tunnel serves A and B so the tile is the same in both. Tilings correspond exactly to one cut tunnel per cell (also verified: `w10_cuts.py` result is consistent, and here Sec. 3).

Attempts to find a cheaper NO solution, one by one, all of which are absorbed by the counting:
* moving a permanent guest z out of its host cluster: every host member (p/2 of them) gets two more edits, so `r_c >= p/2*2`; absorbed in `r_c`.
* a Latin member in state O or X (2 or 3 edits): adds to `E_c`; costs 1 unshared, so a chain deviation via O costs 1 for ONE chain, total 2 per cell instead of 1; absorbed.
* the comparator switch member `(z1,y,z2)` in state O (y alone, two deletions): excess 1, lies in `S_rest`; it removes a clause for free-ish and this IS the cheapest NO deviation found by the exact solver (NO instances come out at exactly `k^2 + 1`, Sec. 3). Consistent with the bound: it does not help any chain's `r_c >= 1`.
* inserting/deleting cross-gadget pairs (anchors of different cells, z-z across chains, y-y): they lie in `S_rest` or are non-member hard edits; an insertion between two anchors of different cells forces further edits (singleton cluster tests), and in any case they never reduce a chain's `r_c`.
* deleting hard glue / splitting a proto-clique: Lemma 5.
* cost sharing via double-edited members: members of `H_c` lie in exactly one `Pi`; members of `H_cmp` in none (only their pad pair, unedited). No member is in two regions.

## 3. (c) Exact optimum on the thick instances (all pairs editable)

That minimum is exactly a weighted correlation clustering on the contracted instance: classes = super-vertices and single guest vertices, `e_AB`/`ne_AB` = numbers of edge/non-edge vertex pairs between classes (computed from the real thick graph, so every pad pair, host pair, thin member pair, hard non-edge and tunnel is accounted), cost = sum over class pairs (`ne` if together, `e` if apart) + internal non-edges. ALL class pairs are editable; no assumption about member states; solved EXACTLY by CP-SAT with lazily added transitivity triples (UNSAT/optimal certificates by the solver). ### 3a. p smaller than required
Requested: see the failure mode. Result: not reachable on these instances, and the reason is informative. For k = 1 the proof's requirement is `p >= l + u_max + 2`; with no tile `u_max = 1` and p = 4 = `1+1+2` is already enough, so p = 4 is not "too small". Going further down is blocked by the glue: p = 2 has no room for a pad pair (the proto-clique `K_2` minus its pad is disconnected), and at p = 4 a super-vertex with two matchings (hosts in the YES instance, `u = 2`) has disconnected glue (`K_4` minus two 1-factors is a perfect matching; the builder's assertion `glue disconnected` fires). Probing instead with larger l at p = 4 (NO, no tile): l = 0..7 UNSAT, l = 8 SAT with no split super-vertex: the cheapest deviation is the unsplit one of cost p per chain, never the split. So the p-bound of Lemma 5 is a sufficient condition and far from tight (splitting a proto-clique also pays for every Latin member through it). The failure mode that the proof describes (cheap split of a `K_v` when `p - 1 - u_max <= l`) therefore could not be exhibited; this is a statement about tightness only and does not affect soundness.

## 4. (d) Width bounds (`a15_tw.py`)
Independent construction of the path decomposition of `I(H)` (nodes = members, adjacent iff they share a vertex, as in `analysis_01.md`): bag at coordinate t = members whose interval `[min,max]` of vertex coordinates contains t. I verified VALIDITY by brute force rather than by the cell argument: for every vertex of the thick graph the intervals of the members through it have a common point (Helly for intervals), 0 violations; max span of a member = 2 cells in all runs. Results, k = 1, p = 8, random S: `n=2 (m=14)`: max bag 1296, pw <= 1295; `n=3 (m=26)`: 1296; `n=4 (m=42)`: 1304, pw <= 1303; `n=5 (m=62)`: 1296 (|V| = 722, 1314, 2118, 3118). Independent of n. c = 33, 34, 34, 34 (<= 4p+2 = 34). The notes' 1303 for k = 1 is reproduced.
Proof sketch that this is a proof, not only a measurement (supports audit 14 F2): a member of cell-coordinate t touches coordinates t..t+2 (span <= 2, checked), guests and comparator members sit at the same coordinate 2u+1 in every chain, tunnels are hard pairs and give no edge of `I(H)`; the bag at t is the union over the 2k^2 chains of the members through t, a quantity depending only on p. So pw(I(H)) <= (2k^2)(const p^2) = O(k^6) once p = Theta(k^2); no dependence on n.
Chain lemma extra evidence (`a15_chain.py`, SAT enumeration of all ZE partitions of the thin chain): m = 14, 18, 26, 42 (the m of n = 2, 3, 4): exactly m partitions, with spine words `R^a M L^{m-1-a}`. This agrees with the transfer-matrix certificate.
Pair-maximality (`a15_pm.py`): 0 all-hard induced P3 for (k,n,p) = (1,2,8) and (2,2,10).
u_max (`a15_umax.py`): <= 3 for all k = 1..5, n = 2..4 (worst case: all tiles present).
