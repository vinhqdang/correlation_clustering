\title{Cluster Editing above a $P_3$ packing:\\ W[1]-hardness in the excess and the treewidth of the intersection graph}
\author{Quang-Vinh Dang\\ British University Vietnam, Hung Yen, Vietnam\\ \texttt{vinh.dq4@buv.edu.vn}}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Cluster Editing asks for the fewest edge insertions and deletions that turn a graph into a disjoint union of cliques.
Li, Pilipczuk and Sorge parameterize it by the excess~$\ell$ of the solution size over a modification-disjoint packing~$H$ of induced $P_3$'s, and leave open for which packings the problem is fixed-parameter tractable.
We use the treewidth~$w$ of the intersection graph $I(H)$ of the packing as the structural measure.
Our main result is a negative answer to the question whether $\ell+w$ suffices: the problem is W[1]-hard parameterized by $\ell+w$, and also by $\ell+\mathrm{pw}(I(H))$ and by $\ell+c$, where $c$ is the maximum number of packed $P_3$'s through a vertex, even for pair-maximal packings. Hence there is no algorithm in time $f(\ell,w)n^{O(1)}$ unless $\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W[1]}$, and under ETH none in time $f(\ell,w)n^{o(\sqrt\ell)}$.
The reduction is from Grid Tiling with $\le$; it uses a selection chain of bounded width whose position is released by cutting one of many ``tunnels'', and is computer-assisted at four finite steps, which we list.
The matching upper bounds come from a CSP view: for $\ell=0$ the problem is a constraint satisfaction problem with domain size~$3$ whose constraint scopes are cliques of $I(H)$; for general $\ell$, guessing at most $\ell$ edits gives such a problem with domain size~$5$.
This yields algorithms in time $3^{w+1}n^{O(1)}$ and $5^{w+1}n^{2\ell+O(1)}$, and $n^{\ell}f(\ell,w)\,5^{w+\ell+1}n^{O(1)}$ after a locality argument.
Under ETH there is no $2^{o(w)}n^{O(1)}$ algorithm even for $\ell=0$, and neither $w$ nor $\ell$ alone suffices.
If the components of $I(H)$ have bounded size, the problem is fixed-parameter tractable in $\ell$ and has a kernel.
We isolate a configuration, three pairs of a vertex triple packed by three distinct members (a \emph{soft triangle}), as the source of difficulty: without soft triangles the set of zero-excess solutions is closed under every majority operation, so $\ell=0$ is polynomial for every $c$ and $w$, lock cores have at most two anchors, a far-lift model of the excess is fixed-parameter tractable, and on forests the problem is fixed-parameter tractable in $\ell$ conditionally on a bounded-certificate hypothesis; with soft triangles minimal certificates of the natural branching scheme have unbounded size, and the hardness reduction shows that soft triangles suffice.
For pair-maximal packings we give a normal form of optimal solutions, a dichotomy of inserted pairs into local and non-local ones, and an instance family in which an optimum must delete an edge between members at arbitrary distance in $I(H)$.
The parameters belong to the packing, not to the graph. Exchanging one packed $P_3$ for an induced $P_3$ keeps the size and raises $w$ by at most one; we use such exchanges to analyse two obstructions, and we show that locating a good repacking cannot be done in fixed-parameter time unless $\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W[1]}$.
Still open are hardness for bounded $c$ (our reduction has $c=O(\ell)$), the case $c=2$, and the exact exponent of $n$ under ETH ($n^{o(\sqrt\ell)}$ is excluded, $n^{\ell}$ is achieved).
\end{abstract}
