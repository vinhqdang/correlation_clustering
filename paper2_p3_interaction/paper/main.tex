\begin{abstract}
Cluster Editing asks for the fewest edge insertions and deletions that turn a graph into a disjoint union of cliques.
Li, Pilipczuk and Sorge parameterize it by the excess~$\ell$ of the solution size over a modification-disjoint packing~$H$ of induced $P_3$'s, and leave open for which packings the problem is fixed-parameter tractable.
We use the treewidth~$w$ of the intersection graph $I(H)$ of the packing as the structural measure.
Our main result is a negative answer to the question whether $\ell+w$ suffices: the problem is W[1]-hard parameterized by $\ell+w$, and also by $\ell+\mathrm{pw}(I(H))$ and by $\ell+c$, where $c$ is the maximum number of packed $P_3$'s through a vertex, even for pair-maximal packings. Hence there is no algorithm in time $f(\ell,w)n^{O(1)}$ unless $\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W[1]}$, and under ETH none in time $f(\ell,w)n^{o(\sqrt\ell)}$.
The reduction is from Grid Tiling with $\le$; it uses a selection chain of bounded width whose position is released by cutting one of many ``tunnels'', and it is computer-assisted at four finite steps, which we list (the chain lemma for chains of length $m\ge10$ rests on a finite computation); its counting core and several structural lemmas are also machine-checked in Lean~4, which does not cover the graph-level statements of the reduction.
The matching upper bounds come from a CSP view: for $\ell=0$ the problem is a constraint satisfaction problem with domain size~$3$ whose scopes are cliques of $I(H)$; for general $\ell$, guessing at most $\ell$ edits gives domain size~$5$. This yields algorithms in time $3^{w+1}n^{O(1)}$ and $5^{w+1}n^{2\ell+O(1)}$, and $n^{\ell}f(\ell,w)5^{w+\ell+1}n^{O(1)}$ after a locality argument. Under ETH there is no $2^{o(w)}n^{O(1)}$ algorithm even for $\ell=0$, and neither $w$ nor $\ell$ alone suffices.
We isolate a configuration, a \emph{soft triangle}, as the source of difficulty: without soft triangles the zero-excess solutions are closed under every majority operation, so $\ell=0$ is polynomial for every $c$ and $w$; the hardness reduction shows that soft triangles suffice. At $\ell=0$ and $c\le2$ a certificate of at most four remote pairs always exists, even with soft triangles.
Further results (structure of optimal solutions, components of $I(H)$, locality of inserted pairs, repacking) are stated in a short section with proofs in the appendices.
Still open are hardness for bounded $c$ (our reduction has $c=O(\ell)$), the case $c=2$, bounded certificates for $\ell\ge1$, and the exact exponent of $n$ under ETH.
\end{abstract}
