\section{Computer verifications and formal verification}\label{app:checks}
The proofs in this paper rely on computation in three places only: (i) statements about explicit small instances were verified by exhaustive enumeration of set partitions, as indicated where they occur; (ii) the finite classifications marked \emph{by computer} (Theorem~\ref{thm:sfA}, Theorem~\ref{thm:CE}, Lemma~\ref{lem:mcP} and Theorem~\ref{thm:MC0}); and (iii) the four computer-assisted steps (CA1)--(CA4) in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:hard}. All other computations reported below are supporting experiments: they test statements that are proved by hand, and no proof uses them. Where two implementations are mentioned, the second was written directly from the definitions (all set partitions, an exact subset dynamic programme, SAT or CP-SAT) without using the structure lemmas.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} The code for all computations and the Lean~4 development are available in the repository \texttt{vinhqdang/correlation\_clustering} on GitHub, directory \texttt{paper2\_p3\_interaction} (subdirectories \texttt{code} and \texttt{lean/P3Proofs}).

\subsection{Exact optima of full thick instances}\label{sec:hexact}
This experiment supports the reduction and is not part of the proof. By Lemma~\ref{lem:hsplit}, every solution with $\mathrm{ex}\le k^2$ leaves each $K_v$ inside one cluster, so $\OPT\le|H|+k^2$ iff the minimum over solutions with every $K_v$ unsplit is at most $|H|+k^2$. That minimum is the optimum of a weighted correlation clustering instance on the \emph{contraction} (classes: the super-vertices and the single guest vertices; for two classes $A,B$ the weights are the numbers of edges and of non-edges of $G$ between them, computed from the real thick graph, so that every pad pair, host pair, thin member pair, hard non-edge and tunnel is counted; plus the internal non-edges of each class). All pairs of classes are editable and nothing is assumed about the states of the members; the problem is solved exactly by CP-SAT with lazily added transitivity constraints. The contraction was validated against the unrestricted exact solver (all pairs of $V(G)$ editable, no contraction) for $k=1$: with one tile $p=8$ both give $\OPT=|H|+1$ ($|H|=3258$), and without a tile ($p=4$) both give $\OPT=|H|+8$ ($\ell=0,\dots,7$ unsatisfiable, $\ell=8$ satisfiable, no split class). Results, with $\mathrm{ex}=\OPT-|H|$ (expected $\mathrm{ex}=k^2$ if Grid Tiling is solvable and $\mathrm{ex}\ge k^2+1$ otherwise):

The largest instance has $|V(G)|=11{,}262$ and $|H|=153{,}498$ ($k=3$). On every instance solved, YES instances have $\mathrm{ex}=k^2$ exactly and NO instances have $\mathrm{ex}=k^2+1$ exactly (the cheapest deviation found by the solver is one comparator guest left alone, one member with two edits). That NO instances sit at exactly $k^2+1$ is an observation on these instances, not a theorem; the proof only uses $\mathrm{ex}\ge k^2+1$. The computation is an end-to-end exact test for $k\ge2$ that the unrestricted solver could not perform; it relies on Lemma~\ref{lem:hsplit}, which is proved by hand and was confirmed against the unrestricted solver for $k=1$.

\subsection{Formal verification in Lean 4}\label{sec:hlean}
Part of the argument was formalised in Lean~4 with Mathlib (see the repository in the paragraph on reproducibility above). All files are free of \texttt{sorry} and of \texttt{native\_decide}; every theorem depends at most on the axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}, as reported by \texttt{\#print axioms}. The finite computations use \texttt{decide +kernel}, which is evaluated by the Lean kernel and does not add \texttt{Lean.ofReduceBool}.

\emph{Machine-checked.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{CSP representation (Section~\ref{sec:csp}).} Clusterings versus cluster graphs and the cost/OPT equivalence; $\mathrm{OPT}\ge|H|$ for a modification-disjoint packing; Observations~\ref{obs:one} and~\ref{obs:two} (a solution with $|S|\le|H|$ has one pair per member and nothing else, $(G,H,0)$ is YES iff the CSP is satisfiable, the constraint of a triple depends only on its owners, owners of a triple pairwise share a vertex; the bound ``at most three owners'' and the treewidth statements are not included); the five-state lemma, in its finite form and in its graph form.
\item \emph{Structural lemmas.} The twin lemma (twins lie in one cluster of every optimum); half-degree and diameter at most $2$ of an optimal cluster, and separation of vertices at distance at least $3$; the lemmas on $M$ (MAX) and on the hub (HUB, in the bounds $\nu(\Lambda_u)\le\ell$, vertex cover of size at most $2\ell$ and $|Y|\le2\ell+1$); the small-merge lemma (SM) in abstract form, with its hypotheses (cross-pair condition, size and soft-degree bounds) taken as assumptions.
\item \emph{Block encoding of Grid Tiling.} The encoding of tiles by slot and separator groups, the comparator clauses, and the equivalence of the comparator conditions on the A-chains and B-chains with the Grid Tiling constraints (the last step of Proposition~\ref{prop:hTR}, $\Rightarrow$).
\item \emph{Counting core of Theorem~\ref{thm:hS}.} As an abstract system in the data $h_c$, $E_c$, $q$, $h_{\mathrm{in}}$, $|S_{\mathrm{rest}}|-|H_{\mathrm{cmp}}|$, $\mathrm{ex}$, with the hypotheses $\sum_ch_c=h_{\mathrm{in}}+2q$, $r_c\ge\max\{1,h_c\}$ and $\mathrm{ex}+q=\sum_cr_c+(|S_{\mathrm{rest}}|-|H_{\mathrm{cmp}}|)$: the bound $\mathrm{ex}\ge\max\{q,2k^2-q\}\ge k^2$, the inequality $\mathrm{ex}\ge|Z|+h_{\mathrm{in}}+q+\sum_{c\notin Z}E_c$, the equality case ($q=k^2$, $Z=\emptyset$, $h_{\mathrm{in}}=0$, all $E_c=0$, all $h_c=1$, $|S_{\mathrm{rest}}|=|H_{\mathrm{cmp}}|$), and that the bound is attained by a consistent system. The summation must run over $c\notin Z$ only: with $\sum_cE_c$ in its place the inequality fails for $k=1$, $h=(0,0)$, $E=(1,1)$, $\mathrm{ex}=2$, where the right-hand side would be $4$.
\item \emph{Chain lemma, finite version.} Lemma~\ref{lem:hchain} for $m=7,8,9$ only: every $P_a$ is ZE, and every ZE partition of $\mathrm{OH}_m$ is one of the $P_a$ with spine word $R^aML^{m-1-a}$ (kernel computation).
\item \emph{Transfer-matrix algebra.} The characteristic polynomial $x^4(x-1)^2$ of the $6\times6$ matrix, $M^4(M-1)^2=0$, $M^t=M^4+(t-4)(M^5-M^4)$ for $t\ge4$, and the affine-growth lemma: if $N_m=m$ at two consecutive arguments from the stable range on, then $N_m=m$ for all $m$ in that range.
\item \emph{Parameter arithmetic.} $p\le k^2+6$, $c\le4p+2\le4k^2+26$, and the no-split inequality of Lemma~\ref{lem:hsplit} ($x(p-x-u)>k^2$ under the stated bounds).
\end{itemize}

\emph{Not machine-checked.} Theorem~\ref{thm:hS} at graph level; the blow-up lemma; host profiles (Lemma~\ref{lem:hprof}); tunnel release at graph level; the region bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:hregion}); pathwidth and pair-maximality of the reduction instance; the chain lemma for $m\ge10$; the link between the graph $\mathrm{OH}_m$ and the transfer matrix (so the steps (CA1)--(CA4) remain computer-assisted, with Lean covering only $m\le9$ and the matrix algebra); the dynamic programmes and running-time theorems; the ETH statements; and the reduction as a whole (Theorem~\ref{thm:hard}). The hypotheses of the counting core are taken as given, so the Lean result certifies the arithmetic of Theorem~\ref{thm:hS}, not the claim that the instance $I_{\mathrm{tile}}$ satisfies them.
