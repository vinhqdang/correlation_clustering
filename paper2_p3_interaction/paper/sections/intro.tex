\section{Introduction}
Cluster Editing asks for the fewest edge insertions and deletions that turn a graph $G$ into a disjoint union of cliques.
Every induced $P_3$ must be hit by an edit, so a packing $H$ of induced $P_3$'s that pairwise share no vertex pair (a \emph{modification-disjoint} packing) gives the lower bound $|H|$ on the solution size.
Li, Pilipczuk and Sorge~\cite{LPS23} parameterize by the \emph{excess} $\ell$ over this bound (problem CEaMP): is there a solution of size at most $|H|+\ell$?
They show NP-hardness already for $\ell=0$, with every vertex in at most $23$ packed $P_3$'s, and an $n^{2\ell+O(1)}$ algorithm when every vertex lies in at most two packed $P_3$'s; whether the latter case is fixed-parameter tractable is open~\cite{LPS23}.
For vertex-disjoint packings the problem is fixed-parameter tractable in $\ell$~\cite{BFK18}.

The question of this paper is which measurable \emph{interaction structure} of $H$ makes the local repair choices (one edit per packed $P_3$, in three ways) combine into a global solution in $f(\ell,w)\,n^{O(1)}$ time.
We take $w=\tw(I(H))$, where the intersection graph $I(H)$ has the packed $P_3$'s as nodes and joins two of them when they share a vertex.

\subsection{Results}
Our first theorem collects the results of Sections~\ref{sec:csp} and~\ref{sec:limits}.
\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let $(G,H,\ell)$ be an instance of CEaMP, $w=\tw(I(H))$, and assume a tree decomposition of $I(H)$ of width $w$ is given.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{CSP representation.} $(G,H,0)$ is a YES instance iff a constraint satisfaction problem with one variable of domain $\{1,2,3\}$ per packed $P_3$ is satisfiable; every constraint has at most three variables, and its scope is a clique of $I(H)$. For $\ell>0$, for each of the at most $n^{2\ell}$ sets $U$ of at most $\ell$ hard pairs there is such a problem with domain size $5$ (the five edit sets of a packed $P_3$ that leave it a cluster graph, Lemma~\ref{lem:five}) and unary costs, and $(G,H,\ell)$ is a YES instance iff for some $U$ its minimum cost is at most $|H|+\ell-|U|$.
\item[(b)] \emph{Algorithms.} CEaMP is solvable in time $3^{w+1}n^{O(1)}$ for $\ell=0$ and in time $5^{w+1}n^{2\ell+O(1)}$ for general $\ell$. The latter is XP in $\ell$ with single-exponential dependence on $w$; it is not a fixed-parameter algorithm.
\item[(c)] \emph{ETH lower bound.} Unless ETH fails, there is no $2^{o(w)}n^{O(1)}$ algorithm for $\ell=0$, even if every vertex lies in at most $23$ packed $P_3$'s.
\item[(d)] \emph{Neither parameter suffices alone.} CEaMP is NP-hard when $w=0$ (with $\ell$ part of the input) and, by~\cite{LPS23}, when $\ell=0$.
\item[(e)] \emph{Soft triangles.} Call a vertex triple a \emph{soft triangle} if its three pairs are packed by three distinct members. If $(G,H)$ has no soft triangle (in particular if $I(H)$ is triangle-free, e.g.\ a forest), then the set of zero-excess state vectors is closed under every majority operation; consequently $(G,H,0)$ is decidable in polynomial time for every $c$ and $w$, and every lock core has at most two anchors.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Parts (a) and (b) are Theorems~\ref{thm:l0} and~\ref{thm:A}; (c) and (d) are Theorem~\ref{thm:eth} and Proposition~\ref{prop:w0}; (e) is Theorem~\ref{thm:sfA} with Corollaries~\ref{cor:sfpoly} and~\ref{cor:sfcore} (Section~\ref{sec:soft}). The CSP view and the dynamic program are standard once the interaction structure is identified (Observation~\ref{obs:two}); (c) is a consequence of the reduction of~\cite{LPS23}.

The remaining results concern the structure of optimal solutions and the question whether $(\ell,w)$ suffices.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Bounded components} (Section~\ref{sec:K}). If $s$ is the maximum number of vertices spanned by a connected component of $I(H)$, CEaMP is solvable in time $1.62^{(\binom s2+1)\ell}n^{O(1)}+n3^s$ and has a kernel with at most $(s^2-s+2)\ell$ vertices; in particular $I(H)$ a perfect matching is fixed-parameter tractable in $\ell$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:K}). This is the reduction rule of van Bevern, Froese and Komusiewicz~\cite{BFK18} applied to components of $I(H)$; we verify its hypotheses and the counting at the level of components, and cite the soundness of the rule~\cite[Lemma~6.3]{BFK18}, the algorithm of B\"ocker~\cite{Boe12} and the $2k$ kernel of Chen and Meng~\cite{CM12}. A variant for components of arbitrary size leaves at most $2\ell$ components (Theorem~\ref{thm:three}).
\item \emph{Normal form and an $n^{\ell}$ algorithm} (Sections~\ref{sec:optimal} and~\ref{sec:nl}). For a pair-maximal packing (no induced $P_3$ has all three pairs hard), in an optimal solution of size at most $|H|+\ell$, every cluster that contains an end of a hard edit is connected, of size at most $2D+\ell+1$ and of bounded hard degree, and one end of a hard edit determines at most $Z^2$ candidates for the other, where $Z=O(w+\ell)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:cand}). This gives an algorithm in time $(n+1)^{\ell}(6w+9\ell+7)^{2\ell}5^{w+\ell+1}n^{O(1)}$ for every packing (Theorem~\ref{thm:nl}), halving the exponent of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, and a fixed-parameter algorithm for a stronger parameter $\omega$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:omega}). The parameter $\omega$ is not bounded by any function of $(\ell,w)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:expander}).
\item \emph{Locality of inserted pairs} (Section~\ref{sec:locality}). Every inserted hard pair of an optimal solution of a pair-maximal packing has a middle vertex and is either of type T1 (two soft steps in distinct packed $P_3$'s; local in a decomposition of $I(H)$) or of type T2 (one kept hard step). Merges of two pieces joined by no soft pair are of type T1 only (Corollary~\ref{cor:softfree}); merges joined by a soft pair can be of type T2 (examples with $n=7$ and $n=8$, Proposition~\ref{prop:cex}). For every $m\ge3$ there is an instance with $w=1$, $c=2$, $\ell=1$ in which every optimum deletes a hard edge whose ends lie in packed $P_3$'s at distance $m-1$ in $I(H)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:FAR}).
\item \emph{Soft triangles are the source of hardness} (Section~\ref{sec:soft}). Besides Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(e): for soft-triangle-free packings a far-lift model of the excess (any set of at most $\ell$ far hard edits and no other deviation) is solvable in time $6^{\ell}n^{O(1)}$ for every $c$ and $w$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:U}), via the fact that unit lists have Helly number two (Lemma~\ref{lem:sfH2}); on forests the full problem is fixed-parameter tractable in $\ell$ \emph{conditionally} on a bounded-certificate hypothesis, which affects only the running time (Theorem~\ref{thm:F}); the natural abstract hitting model of the problem on forests is fixed-parameter tractable but not faithful (Propositions~\ref{prop:RM2} and~\ref{prop:hub}). A certificate-branching scheme has a fixed-parameter leaf test (Theorem~\ref{thm:L}) and bounded minimal certificates at $\ell=0$ without soft triangles (Theorem~\ref{thm:CB0}), but with soft triangles minimal certificates of unbounded size exist already at $c=2$, $w=2$, $\ell=0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:CE}). An abstract linked-pair path-hitting problem is W[1]-hard (Theorem~\ref{thm:B}), but without soft triangles tunnel releases cannot realise it (Theorem~\ref{thm:C}).
\item \emph{Repacking} (Section~\ref{sec:repack}). The parameters belong to the packing: replacing a packed $P_3$ by an induced $P_3$ keeps $|H|$ and raises $w$ by at most one, with a pair-maximal repair costing at most $\ell$ more (Lemma~\ref{lem:rpex}); every hard edit of an optimum lies in an induced $P_3$ and is either bridged (local in every decomposition) or absorbable by one exchange (Lemma~\ref{lem:rpwit}); no hard edit has two uncovered ends. The far-deletion family of Proposition~\ref{prop:FAR} has intrinsic excess one, but after one exchange $I(H^*)$ is a cycle and its only hard edit is at an uncovered vertex; the spine family has excess zero and $\tw(I(H^*))\le2$ after one exchange and one augmentation (Propositions~\ref{prop:rpFAR} and~\ref{prop:rpSp}). A one-sided algorithm in time $5^{w+1}(Z^2+1)^{3\ell(w+1)}n^{O(1)}$ is exact whenever some optimum has only hard edits that are co-bagged or at local hubs (Theorem~\ref{thm:rpPL}); its promise cannot be tested. No polynomial procedure outputs a repacking with excess and width bounded in $\ell$ alone unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:rpN}), bounded-radius augmentation cannot find the good packing (Proposition~\ref{prop:rpaug}), and the naive normal form ``hard edits have an uncovered end'' is false (Proposition~\ref{prop:rpnf}); a corrected normal form is a conjecture (Conjecture~\ref{conj:rpC1}). What is left is the problem of locating the repacking (Open Problem~\ref{op:LOC}).
\end{itemize}
Appendices~\ref{app:structure}--\ref{app:checks} contain further proved statements that are not used in the main text: twins, the structure of the vertices outside the packing and a shrink rule, counting lemmas, the dynamic program for $\omega$, conflict weights and a pruned variant, a family on which reduction on unions of packed $P_3$'s fails, and tunnel gadgets. Appendix~\ref{app:soft} contains the proofs for Section~\ref{sec:soft}, and Appendix~\ref{app:repack} those for Section~\ref{sec:repack}.

\paragraph{What is open.}
\textbf{OPEN:} whether CEaMP is fixed-parameter tractable in $\ell+\tw(I(H))$, or W[1]-hard. This is open already for $w=1$ with tree components of unbounded size. Our algorithms are XP in $\ell$, Theorem~\ref{thm:K} needs bounded components, and no hardness is known. The precise status after Section~\ref{sec:soft} is: the soft-triangle-free case reduces to the bounded-certificate hypothesis BC (Theorem~\ref{thm:F}), and a hardness proof would need soft triangles, at least along the routes that we can analyse (Theorems~\ref{thm:C} and~\ref{thm:CE}); neither direction is proved. Section~\ref{sec:repack} adds the following precise status: the far-deletion family and the spine family are artifacts of the chosen packing (after repacking they have $\tw(I(H^*))\le2$, and the far hard edit is at an uncovered vertex, respectively the excess is $0$), but \emph{locating} the repacking is itself open (Open Problem~\ref{op:LOC}), and it cannot be done with bounds in $\ell$ alone unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:rpN}). The normal form of Section~\ref{sec:optimal} applies to a pair-maximal extension of the given packing, whose intersection graph may have larger treewidth. Section~\ref{sec:open} lists the open problems and summarises, in one table, which result holds for the input packing and which for a pair-maximal extension.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}
van Bevern, Froese and Komusiewicz~\cite{BFK18} parameterize $F$-free editing above packings; for Cluster Editing they obtain fixed-parameter algorithms for vertex-disjoint $P_3$ packings and leave open the modification-disjoint case, which Li, Pilipczuk and Sorge~\cite{LPS23} settle negatively for $\ell=0$ and study for $1/c$-integral packings. Modification-disjoint $P_3$ packings are also used as a lower-bound tool for cluster vertex splitting~\cite{Split24}; for broader context see the survey~\cite{Crespelle23}. To our knowledge, the treewidth of the intersection graph of the packing has not been used as a parameter before. Komusiewicz and Uhlmann~\cite{KU12} show that Cluster Editing is NP-hard on graphs of maximum degree $6$ and has no $2^{o(k)}n^{O(1)}$ algorithm under ETH. The critical-clique (twin) property used in Appendix~\ref{app:structure} goes back to~\cite{Guo09,FLRS07}; the half-degree property of Lemma~\ref{lem:half} is the same single-vertex-move argument. Cubic expanders of linear treewidth are cited from Grohe and Marx~\cite{GM09}.
