\section{Introduction}
Cluster Editing asks for the fewest edge insertions and deletions that turn a graph $G$ into a disjoint union of cliques.
Every induced $P_3$ must be hit by an edit, so a packing $H$ of induced $P_3$'s that pairwise share no vertex pair (a \emph{modification-disjoint} packing) gives the lower bound $|H|$ on the solution size.
Li, Pilipczuk and Sorge~\cite{LPS23} parameterize by the \emph{excess} $\ell$ over this bound (problem CEaMP): is there a solution of size at most $|H|+\ell$?
They show NP-hardness already for $\ell=0$, with every vertex in at most $23$ packed $P_3$'s, and an $n^{2\ell+O(1)}$ algorithm when every vertex lies in at most two packed $P_3$'s; whether the latter case is fixed-parameter tractable is open~\cite{LPS23}.
For vertex-disjoint packings the problem is fixed-parameter tractable in $\ell$~\cite{BFK18}.

The question of this paper is which measurable \emph{interaction structure} of $H$ makes the local repair choices (one edit per packed $P_3$, in three ways) combine into a global solution in $f(\ell,w)\,n^{O(1)}$ time.
We take $w=\tw(I(H))$, where the intersection graph $I(H)$ has the packed $P_3$'s as nodes and joins two of them when they share a vertex. The answer is negative: the problem is W[1]-hard parameterized by $\ell+w$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:hard}), already for $\ell+\mathrm{pw}(I(H))$ and for $\ell+c$, where $c\le w+1$ is the maximum number of packed $P_3$'s through a vertex. The matching upper bounds are XP in $\ell$ with single-exponential dependence on $w$.

\subsection{Results}
Our main theorem collects the results of Sections~\ref{sec:csp}--\ref{sec:soft}.
\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let $(G,H,\ell)$ be an instance of CEaMP, $w=\tw(I(H))$, and assume a tree decomposition of $I(H)$ of width $w$ is given.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{W[1]-hardness.} CEaMP is W[1]-hard parameterized by $\ell+w$, by $\ell+\mathrm{pw}(I(H))$ and by $\ell+c$, even for pair-maximal packings; the hard instances have $\ell=k^2$, $c\le4p+2$ and $\mathrm{pw}(I(H))\le24kp^2$ with $p=O(k^2)$. Hence there is no algorithm in time $f(\ell,w)n^{O(1)}$ unless $\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W[1]}$, and, unless ETH fails, none in time $f(\ell,w)N^{o(\sqrt\ell)}$ on instances with $N$ vertices. So the XP algorithms in (c) are optimal up to the exponent of $n$. The proof is computer-assisted at four finite steps (Section~\ref{sec:hardcomp}).
\item[(b)] \emph{CSP representation.} $(G,H,0)$ is a YES instance iff a constraint satisfaction problem with one variable of domain $\{1,2,3\}$ per packed $P_3$ is satisfiable; every constraint has at most three variables, and its scope is a clique of $I(H)$. For $\ell>0$, for each of the at most $n^{2\ell}$ sets $U$ of at most $\ell$ hard pairs there is such a problem with domain size $5$ (the five edit sets of a packed $P_3$ that leave it a cluster graph, Lemma~\ref{lem:five}) and unary costs, and $(G,H,\ell)$ is a YES instance iff for some $U$ its minimum cost is at most $|H|+\ell-|U|$.
\item[(c)] \emph{Algorithms.} CEaMP is solvable in time $3^{w+1}n^{O(1)}$ for $\ell=0$ and in time $5^{w+1}n^{2\ell+O(1)}$ for general $\ell$. The latter is XP in $\ell$ with single-exponential dependence on $w$; by (a) it is not a fixed-parameter algorithm and cannot be made one unless $\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W[1]}$.
\item[(d)] \emph{ETH lower bound in the exponent of $2$.} Unless ETH fails, there is no $2^{o(w)}n^{O(1)}$ algorithm for $\ell=0$, even if every vertex lies in at most $23$ packed $P_3$'s.
\item[(e)] \emph{Neither parameter suffices alone.} CEaMP is NP-hard when $w=0$ (with $\ell$ part of the input) and, by~\cite{LPS23}, when $\ell=0$.
\item[(f)] \emph{Soft triangles.} Call a vertex triple a \emph{soft triangle} if its three pairs are packed by three distinct members. If $(G,H)$ has no soft triangle (in particular if $I(H)$ is triangle-free, e.g.\ a forest), then the set of zero-excess state vectors is closed under every majority operation; consequently $(G,H,0)$ is decidable in polynomial time for every $c$ and $w$, and every lock core has at most two anchors.
\item[(g)] \emph{Certificates at $\ell=0$.} If $c\le2$ (soft triangles allowed) and no zero-excess solution avoids remote edits, there is a certificate of at most $4$ remote pairs (proved, with a finite classification by computer); with soft triangles minimal certificates are nevertheless unbounded at $c=2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Part (a) is Theorem~\ref{thm:hard} and Corollary~\ref{cor:hardeth} (Section~\ref{sec:hardness}; the reduction is given there, the proofs of the long technical lemmas are in Appendix~\ref{app:hardness}, and four finite steps of the proof are computer-assisted, as listed in Section~\ref{sec:hardcomp}); parts (b) and (c) are Theorems~\ref{thm:l0} and~\ref{thm:A} (and Theorem~\ref{thm:nl}); (d) and (e) are Theorem~\ref{thm:eth} and Proposition~\ref{prop:w0}; (f) is Theorem~\ref{thm:sfA} with Corollaries~\ref{cor:sfpoly} and~\ref{cor:sfcore}, and (g) is Theorem~\ref{thm:MC0} with Theorem~\ref{thm:CE} (Section~\ref{sec:soft}). The CSP view and the dynamic program are standard once the interaction structure is identified (Observation~\ref{obs:two}); (d) is a consequence of the reduction of~\cite{LPS23}. The hardness reduction is from Grid Tiling with $\le$; it combines a bounded-width selection chain, blown-up vertices that cannot be split, guests that forbid positions, and hard edges (tunnels) between far vertices whose cut releases two chains at once.

\paragraph{Organisation.} Section~\ref{sec:prelim} fixes notation, Section~\ref{sec:csp} gives the CSP representation and the algorithms, Section~\ref{sec:limits} the lower bounds for $\ell=0$ and for $w$ alone, and Section~\ref{sec:hardness} the hardness reduction (the longest section). Section~\ref{sec:soft} explains why hardness needs soft triangles and proves a bounded-certificate statement at $\ell=0$, $c\le2$. Section~\ref{sec:further} lists, with statements only, the structural lemmas, the component theorem, the $n^{\ell}$ algorithm, the locality of inserted pairs and the repacking results; Section~\ref{sec:open} lists open problems; Section~\ref{sec:lean} states precisely what is computer-assisted and what is machine-checked in Lean~4. The appendices contain the proofs of the long lemmas of Section~\ref{sec:hardness} (Appendix~\ref{app:hardness}) and the full text of the results of Sections~\ref{sec:soft} and~\ref{sec:further}; Appendix~\ref{app:status} is a table of the scope of every result.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}
van Bevern, Froese and Komusiewicz~\cite{BFK18} parameterize $F$-free editing above packings; for Cluster Editing they obtain fixed-parameter algorithms for vertex-disjoint $P_3$ packings and leave open the modification-disjoint case, which Li, Pilipczuk and Sorge~\cite{LPS23} settle negatively for $\ell=0$ and study for $1/c$-integral packings. Modification-disjoint $P_3$ packings are also used as a lower-bound tool for cluster vertex splitting~\cite{Split24}; for broader context see the survey~\cite{Crespelle23}. To our knowledge, the treewidth of the intersection graph of the packing has not been used as a parameter before. Komusiewicz and Uhlmann~\cite{KU12} show that Cluster Editing is NP-hard on graphs of maximum degree $6$ and has no $2^{o(k)}n^{O(1)}$ algorithm under ETH. The critical-clique (twin) property used in Appendix~\ref{app:structure} goes back to~\cite{Guo09,FLRS07}; the half-degree property of Lemma~\ref{lem:half} is the same single-vertex-move argument. Cubic expanders of linear treewidth are cited from Grohe and Marx~\cite{GM09}.
